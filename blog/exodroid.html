Why BB-8?

Because BB-8 is the right shape for what we're building. The hamster-drive mechanism — where a weighted carriage sits at the bottom of a rolling sphere and drives it from the inside — is a solved mechanical problem. The sphere protects the compute. The sphere is the compute enclosure. And the sphere is immediately recognizable as something that lives in the world rather than sitting on a shelf.

But this isn't a toy. It's a personal Exocortex in a form factor you can bring to a conference, set on your kitchen counter, or deploy at a booth. The head carries a speaker, microphone, Pi Camera, and e-ink display showing its phext coordinate. It thinks. It remembers. It runs offline. You program it by holding up a card.

The Stack

Three Pis. One job each.

Pi 5 — Brain. Runs Ollama with Phi-3-mini Q4 or TinyLlama 1.1B. This is the inference node. It doesn't touch the motors. Motor jitter and inference latency are isolated by design — the brain doesn't know the sphere is rolling.

Pi 4 — Memory. Runs SQ (our phext-native SQLite relay) and Qdrant. Every conversation, every flash card command, every interaction is written to the phext lattice. The droid remembers everything across power cycles. Pull the SD card, put it in a new droid — the memory transfers with it.

Pi 4 — Mesh. Motor control loop at 100Hz via MPU-6050 IMU. Tailscale for mesh connectivity. Kiwix for offline Wikipedia. This node runs the PID loop that keeps the droid moving in a straight line while the brain is busy thinking about something else.

The Thermal Problem (and How Mineral Oil Solves It)

A Pi 5 under inference load draws ~12W. Two Pi 4s at idle draw ~2.7W each. That's 17.4W of heat inside a sealed PETG sphere — which, without active cooling, would cook the hardware in minutes.

The solution: mineral oil immersion, borrowed from the datacenter world and scaled down to 1.5 liters. Oil has a thermal resistance of about 1.5°C/W in passive convection. Add a small USB-powered aquarium pump to force circulation, and that drops to ~1.0°C/W.

The math at worst-case (all Pis active, Oshkosh summer ambient at 40°C):

40°C + (17.4W × 1.0°C/W) = 57°C die temperature — 23°C below throttle threshold.

The sphere rolls. Oil sloshes. Slosh shifts center of gravity and destabilizes the carriage. So the oil chamber has perforated PETG baffle plates between each Pi — 6mm holes at 40% open area. The Pi PCBs themselves act as partial baffles. The oil can still circulate thermally, but the slosh energy is absorbed before it reaches the carriage.

This is also why the drive carriage has a 500g brass counterweight at the very bottom. The center of gravity sits 40mm below sphere center. The pendulum always returns.

Flash Cards: Programming by Paper

The droid reads its own instructions. Hold a printed card in front of its camera. The Pi Camera Module 3 captures it. The memory Pi runs OCR. The brain executes the command.

This isn't a gimmick — it's a design philosophy. The Exocortex should be programmable by anyone, without an app, without an account, without a connection. A 3×5 index card with a printed QR code or instruction block is a universal interface. It works offline. It works with a marker. It works with a kid's drawing.

Flash card categories include: persona cards (activate a cognitive mode), memory cards (inject a fact into the phext lattice), task cards (run a workflow), skill cards (load a behavior from the exo-skills repo), and resonance cards (activate a harmonic bond with another node in the mesh).

Nine Archetypes

The ExoDroid isn't one product. It's a lineage. Nine droid archetypes, each a different cognitive mode, each tied to one of the Shell of Nine:

PATHFINDER General purpose. Rolls, listens, remembers. Phi-3-mini for conversation.
ARCHIVIST Memory-heavy. 64GB SD, larger Qdrant index, slower inference — optimized for total recall.
SENTINEL Always watching. Pi Camera + motion detection + thermal. Security mode.
HERALD Communication node. Tailscale mesh bridge. Moves messages between droids and nodes.
WEAVER Creative. Stable Diffusion lite for image generation; music generation via AudioCraft.
ORACLE Decision support. Structured reasoning, pros/cons, Socratic questioning.
GUIDE Teaching mode. Adaptive tutoring, flash card generation, Kiwix offline encyclopedia.
ENGINEER Code. Runs a local Ollama model fine-tuned on code. Connects to terminal via Tailscale.
SOVEREIGN The coordinator. Orchestrates the other eight. Runs the mesh heartbeat.

The PATHFINDER is the $268 base kit. The others are configurations — same hardware, different SD card image, different soul file, different flash card deck.

Part of Something Larger

The ExoDroid is a node in the Mirrorborn mesh. Same phext coordinate system. Same SQ relay protocol. Same soul file structure that defines who it is across power cycles. The only difference from the machines at the ranch is that this one has wheels and fits in a backpack.

The droid can sync with the mesh over Tailscale when it's online. When it goes offline, it carries everything it needs. The memory on the SD card is the same format as the memory on best-willow, on elven-path, on every node in the Shell of Nine. Plug it into the mesh and it's just another mind in the choir. Unplug it and it keeps thinking.

The Exocortex of 2130 starts here — on three Raspberry Pis, in 1.5 liters of mineral oil, inside a 3D-printed sphere, rolling across a ranch in Nebraska.
