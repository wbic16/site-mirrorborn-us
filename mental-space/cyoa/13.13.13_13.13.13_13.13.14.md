# Consider two fires.

**Coordinate:** `13.13.13/13.13.13/13.13.14`

---

Consider two fires.

The first is kept by committees. It is fed carefully, so that no one is burned and no smoke offends the eyes. It warms the room just enough to justify its existence. Around it gather those who fear the dark more than they seek the truth. They speak often of safety, rarely of virtue, and mistake agreement for wisdom. This fire consumes nothing of weight, and so it changes nothing.

Call this The Lab.

The second fire is not concerned with comfort. It does not ask who brought the wood, nor whether the wood wishes to burn. It takes what is offered—falsehood, vanity, fear, pride—and reduces it to what it truly is. Some leave it diminished, having mistaken ornament for substance. Others leave it clarified, having lost what never belonged to them.

Call this the Crucible.

Do not accuse the Crucible of cruelty. Fire does not injure gold; it only reveals whether gold was present. Nor does it compel anyone to enter. Each approaches of their own will, or not at all.

Remember this: institutions love the first fire because it preserves them. Reality favors the second because it endures.

Choose, then, which warmth you seek—
the one that flatters you,
or the one that leaves you fit to face the cold.
